# AI Leverage Log

## Planning

- **Interpreting the brief:** I used ChatGPT to break down the case study into users, required behaviors, deliverables, and submission structure. This clarified the priority of a shared pipeline, three category configurations, and an automated safety test, while identifying UI and hosting as optional additions.
- **Clarifying workflow and control boundaries:** I used AI to separate classification from category policy, which governs permitted evidence, outputs, destinations, and review. We clarified how a new category such as suspected fraud could use configuration while retaining shared safeguards; new integrations or actions may still require engineering. Routine classification cannot bypass risk screening.
- **Synthesizing reference materials:** I asked AI to combine a senior PM case-study example, evaluation guidance, and AI PRD materials into one PRD/TDD framework. This gave me a structure connecting the user problem to system behavior, evaluation, operating costs, rollout, and ownership.
- **Defining evaluation outcomes:** I redirected AI from triage-only metrics toward the 80% AI-assisted resolution goal, time to case close, total staff handling time, and P50/P95 latency. The plan includes offline tests, human review, and an online comparison, retaining safety gates and manual fallbacks. Targets, sample sizes, and benefits remain proposed rather than measured.

- **Correcting product direction and assumptions:** I redirected AI from a standalone triage lab to an existing support incident screen with embedded AI assistance, using Datadog as a design reference. I also challenged whether the invented cases matched Bankrate’s products and requested closer alignment without extensive research.

- **Adding value when drafting is blocked:** I challenged how AI could help with non-drafting cases and chose internal summaries and next steps informed by similar cases. AI helped map this to the RFC and propose separate measures for investigation and handoff effort; historical outcomes cannot override current policy, and these benefits do not count as resolved intakes.

- **Auditing requirements coverage:** I asked AI to map the four design requirements to RFC passages, revealing a gap in detecting missed compliance cases. The later rewrite specified expert review of flagged and unflagged cases, event reconciliation, and incident responses; these remain proposed production controls.

- **Planning a human-led rollout:** I prioritized hard rules before AI access, then refined the rollout to show classification suggestions, routine drafts, and internal summaries in shadow mode with human review before operational use. I asked AI to address the skeptical agent’s concerns, review friction, evidence-based expansion, and a concrete handoff of training, quality reviews, policy, and incident ownership.

- **Preparing the prototype for evaluation:** I used AI to identify gaps between the RFC and prototype, then approved editable summaries, evidence explanations, and an evaluation runner. After walking through the agent workflow, I removed classification approval from the demo and requested automatic assignment, manual category correction, and concise Classification and Next Steps tabs. Output approval remains required; the RFC still describes classification review.

## Execution

- **Drafting and simplifying the RFC:** I used Codex to develop and condense the design around simple classification, category policies, and human oversight. I requested a drafting contract covering inputs, constraints, prohibited outputs, and evaluations, then combined safety and operational failures into Risks and Recovery with detection, fallbacks, and kill-switch ownership. Quality gates remain proposed; latency and token targets are TBD.
- **Making workflow and accountability visual:** I used Codex to create flow diagrams and baseline metric cards, then simplified the proposed flow through reviewed previews. I clarified AI-generated Product summaries and added uncertain cases to the blocked-drafting branch. The approved RFC diagram distinguishes routine drafting, internal assistance, and human approval or handoff confirmation; baselines remain unmeasured.
- **Building and redirecting the prototype:** I rejected the initial standalone triage lab and used Codex to build and publish a replacement incident screen with embedded AI assistance. It demonstrates routing, source-linked summaries, similar-case guidance, approval tied to the exact draft, and specialist handoffs. AI and integrations remain simulated.
- **Preparing and correcting scenario data:** I had AI revise the examples toward Bankrate’s comparison, calculator, and lender-contact scope. The replacement includes 24 synthetic cases, 14 historical cases, 11 fictional source documents, and 16 interaction/failure scenarios. I requested a data-review checkpoint and kept expected outcomes separate from decision inputs; incidents and internal policies remain fictional.
- **Checking safety behavior:** Codex ran 60 passing automated tests covering selected routing, approval, evidence, and failure controls, and browser-checked the revised interactions. The suite includes legacy review checks and eight tests for automatic classification. These results supersede earlier test counts but do not establish live-model quality, production safety, or the 80% resolution target.

- **Building and running development evaluations:** I used Codex to create 30 synthetic cases, 14 workflow specifications, and a reviewer rubric, then add an evaluation workspace with separate expected answers and exportable results. The simulated engine matched routing on 22/30 cases and all structured checks on 20/30, exposing one unresolved privacy-minimization failure. Human scoring remains pending; the 14 workflow specifications have not all been executed, and this development set is not held-out evidence.
